DE Flagship Briefs

Five Level A experiences, four for DE's services and one, last, for the AI domain, each answering the Phase 2 questions in full and each carrying a different fingerprint. Nothing here is code; every brief ends in the checklist Joe signs before its materials are made.

Set at Joe's direction, 2026-09-01: DE is a Managed Security Service Provider and an IT Managed Services company. The four service briefs are developed first and to the highest finish. AI material is wanted, so Automation + AI stays as Brief 5, built only after the service flagships and never given more prominence in the information architecture than managed security and managed IT.

Two route defaults are proposed below (Briefs 3 and 5). Approving a brief approves its route unless a different one is named.

Brief 1 of 5

Homepage — The Environment Wakes Up, rebuilt

Route / · replaces the current homepage only when it clears every gate · the previous homepage stays reachable during rollout

Purpose
Change what the visitor believes DE is — from "an IT vendor" to "the company that makes the whole environment work as one system." Everything else on the page serves that one belief.
Audience
Arizona owner-operators and office managers in law, CPA, medical, real estate and veterinary practices. Time-poor, vendor-weary, responsible for something that breaks at 8 a.m.
Belief before
"IT providers are interchangeable ticket-takers. Mine is fine until it isn't."
Belief after
"My environment could work as one designed system — and DE is the company that does exactly that, here, with named people."
Signature move
The Single Line. One continuous stroke — the DE thread — that the visitor's scroll draws through a cutaway of a real business premises (front desk, exam rooms, server closet, phones, cloud, people). Systems don't snap together; they are threaded. The line persists as the page's connective device and closes the loop at the end. It is the redesigned execution of the transformation idea and is not the environment map or a margin folio.
Peak
Scene 4, pinned (2.0): the thread completes its circuit through the cutaway and the whole premises reads as one system for the first time — the "wakes up" moment, drawn by the visitor's own hand.
Quiet moment
The authored silence after the evidence field (scene 3): the fractured premises held, no copy arriving, one line of type: "Each one works. The environment doesn't."
Mobile
A designed five-moment sequence — arrival, fracture, the thread, proof, close — full-viewport states, the cutaway simplified to three rooms, the thread drawn in one vertical pass.
Seams
Opens under the real site nav; closes into the real site footer. The scenes sit on hard-cut grounds; no rounded islands.

Brief 2 of 5

ProActive Ecosystem — a living operating cadence

Route /solutions/proactive-ecosystem · replaces the #166 treatment when ready; every existing word and link stays

Purpose
Make "operated, not watched" tangible: technology becoming an operating system over time.
Audience
The same owner-operators, now comparing plans — plus the office manager who will live with the cadence.
Belief before
"A plan is a bundle of tools with a price."
Belief after
"A plan is a cadence that runs my environment — designed before defended, monitored before it breaks, measured to a roadmap I can read."
Signature move
The cadence that keeps time. Observe → Decide → Protect → Improve, drawn as one operating loop on a held stage; scroll advances the loop, and each service surfaces at the exact moment it becomes operationally relevant — monitoring during Observe, the roadmap during Decide, controls during Protect, the QBR during Improve. Time and rhythm are the grammar; nothing assembles spatially.
Peak
The end of the first full revolution: the loop starts its second turn and the environment is measurably better than the turn before — a real, published improvement metric from the roadmap structure, not invented telemetry.
Quiet moment
The published rates, printed still and exact. No counters.
Mobile
The loop as a vertical rhythm — one revolution, four full-viewport beats, larger type, no lateral travel.
Seams
Amplification stays additive: reduced-motion visitors and crawlers get the page as it is today.

Brief 3 of 5

How We Protect — layered, adaptive, continuous

Route default proposal: elevate /solutions/unified-security (already in the sitemap) rather than add a route · alternative: /how-we-protect

Purpose
Replace the abstract word "cybersecurity" with a visible mechanism: protection as layers around a real business.
Audience
Owners who have been told to "get security" by an insurer, a client contract, or a near miss.
Belief before
"Security is a product you buy once."
Belief after
"Security is layers that adapt and never stop — identity, endpoint, network, email, backup, security operations — and each layer answers a specific threat."
Signature move
The enclosure. One environment held on stage through the whole experience; scroll closes six protective boundaries around it in order, each visibly answering a named threat from the evidence register. No dashboard, no neon wall, no illustrative telemetry presented as real.
Peak
An attack path attempted against the finished enclosure — phishing → credential → lateral movement → encryption — failing layer by layer under the visitor's scroll.
Quiet moment
A.R.S. §18-552's 45-day notification requirement, typeset plainly on paper.
Mobile
Layers accumulate as full-viewport states; the attack path simplified to three beats.

Cyber Risk Assessment — unknown to roadmap

Route /assessment · the conversion flagship · ends at the booking flow

Purpose
Make the assessment itself understandable so sales copy doesn't have to carry it. The page is the product demo.
Audience

Belief before

"It's a real inspection with findings I can act on with anyone — and the obvious first step."

Quiet moment

Mobile

Brief 5 of 5 · last, after the four service flagships

Automation + AI — manual work to orchestrated systems

Route default proposal: create /solutions/automation-ai (no page exists today) · one of fourteen domains, contained · built only after Briefs 1–4 ship · never more prominence in the IA than managed security and managed IT

Purpose
Show what automation actually changes in a small business: handoffs, not headcount. AI as a capability DE operates inside the same secured, managed environment, not a product line of its own.
Audience
Existing and prospective managed-services clients drowning in re-keyed data, approval chains and "who has the file"; skeptical of AI theater.
Belief before
"Automation and AI are hype, or a way to replace my people."
Belief after
"Automation removes handoffs from my processes, AI is contained inside boundaries DE sets and secures, and a person still approves what matters."
Signature move
The collapse. A split stage: left, a real business process drawn with people at every handoff; right, the same process orchestrated. Scroll collapses the many handoffs into one automated path while one human approval gate stays lit throughout and is the last thing standing.
Peak
The pin (2.0) where the last handoffs collapse and the two stages become one path with a single lit gate.
Quiet moment
The approval gate alone on the stage, with the sentence that AI is one contained domain of fourteen.
Mobile
Before/after as two vertical states with the collapse as one transition; the gate persistent at the top.
