## The one finding that governs everything else

The storyboard's persistent visual system is **a business environment**:
people, computers, cloud, email, phones, files, network, vendors, occupying one
world that connects, drifts, corrects, reorganizes, grows the eight blocks,
expands into capabilities, forks into three arrangements, and returns aligned.

**#186 has no environment.** Its persistent objects are three: the course line,
the bearing gauge, and the tilt of the page. Everything else in it is
typography and, in acts 4–6, three constructions (a waypoint list, a card rail,
a button row). So the passage cannot be *converted* into the storyboard by
re-timing or re-styling its acts; the storyboard needs an object #186 never
had. The minimum structural change is therefore one addition, not a rebuild:
**put one persistent environment behind the passage and make its acts drive
that environment's states.** The passage's own best moves then land on
something.

## 3 · What currently breaks storyboard continuity

1. **No environment.** The spine's whole method is "the opening fragments
   become the architecture become the security model become the operating
   environment become the assessment". #186 has nothing that can do that; its
   acts share a design language, not an object.
2. **The eight-block rail** is a card carousel: a new construction that arrives
   from the side and leaves. The spine forbids exactly that ("don't suddenly
   show an eight-item cybersecurity diagram").
3. **The waypoint list** is a list; the spine wants the environment to pass
   through the states.
4. **The pathway buttons** are a button row; the spine wants the same
   environment to fork.
5. **The paper chapter** is a plate change (graphite → paper → graphite). Not
   fatal: it can remain as a *light state* of the world (the principle lands
   in clean light), but it must not read as a section cut.
6. **The final frame does not return.** The spine ends where it began; #186
   ends on the refusals with no camera.
7. **The copy of Act 2** ("You are paying for the hour before anyone knows
   whose problem it is") argues cost; the spine's Act 2 argues "nobody is
   steering the whole thing". Related, not the same beat.

## 4 · The minimum structural changes

In order, each one small, none a wholesale rebuild:

1. **Add the persistent environment as a fixed stage behind the passage**
   (one WebGL or SVG world, built in code, driven by the engine's published
   act progress). The passage's DOM acts stay as they are structurally: pinned
   stages, cues, the same fonts and copy hierarchy.
2. **Move the tilt from the stages to the world.** Same 1.2°, same 900 ms
   roll, same trigger; the environment drifts and corrects, the type stays
   level.
3. **Rewrite the copy of Acts 1–2** to the spine's beats (four lines change).
4. **Replace the waypoint list with world states + captions** (Act 4). The six
   words stay.
5. **Replace the rail with emergence** (Act 5): the seven blocks form from the
   environment's own parts; the band becomes the floor-wide continuous layer.
   The heading stays.
6. **Add Acts 6–7** as two further states of the same world (capabilities
   unlocked; navigation), each with at most three lines of copy.
7. **Bind the entry door and the three pathways to fork states** (Acts 8–9);
   keep the pathway detail as the evidence layer beneath.
8. **Play the refusals on the aligned world and return the camera to the
   door** (Act 10 + final frame); the gauge reads 360°.

Everything above is transformation of one system. Items 1–2 are the flagship
prototype's entire scope of *new* engineering; items 3–8 are conversions that
follow once the world exists and Quality Gate A has passed.

## 5 · What genuinely requires a new asset

**Nothing generated.** The environment is code (geometry, materials, light,
paths); the type is DOM; the stills that stand in for it before the library
loads, under reduced motion and without WebGL are rendered *from* the world by
the build's own script. The only assets outside code in the whole spine are
later and real: the principal and team photography for the proof act (a
shoot, never generated) and sanitized product captures for the proof act. The
asset bible carries two kie.ai *candidates* with defined roles (a defocused
dawn beyond the window; a graphite material study) and recommends neither for
the prototype; they would be revisited only if Quality Gate A names flatness
that code cannot fix.

## The flagship prototype this implies

The first turning point of the spine, which is also #186's own best moment
with the object it was missing: **Act 1 (disconnected) → Act 2 (what's
underneath) → Act 3 (the heading is wrong; the principle; the world corrects;
DE enters) → the first frame of Act 4 (DE sees the whole environment).** About
four viewport-heights, 20–25 seconds at a normal scroll, zero generated
assets. Its success test is Quality Gate A; if it fails, the environment is
wrong, not the storyboard, and we fix the environment before anything expands.
