# Graphite Illumination
An algorithmic philosophy for the Digerati Experts Version B plates.

Graphite Illumination treats light as infrastructure. Its images are not
pictures of things but sections through a dark architecture: long filaments of
near-parallel light travelling a graphite void, converging on vanishing points
the way conduit converges on a riser. The computational substrate is a seeded
value-noise field sampled at two octaves; every filament reads the field as a
quiet perturbation on an otherwise engineered trajectory, so the work sits
deliberately between drafting and weather. Nothing in the frame is drawn by
hand and nothing is random twice: one seed, one plate, reproducible forever.

The movement's single governing parameter is coherence. At low coherence the
field wins: strokes shatter into disconnected dashes, clusters drift apart,
and the frame reads as interference, a system talking over itself. As
coherence rises the engineered trajectory wins: dashes fuse into beams, beams
agree on a vanishing point, and a lattice emerges that was implicit in the
noise all along. The arc from fragmentation to system is the conceptual seed
carried silently inside every plate; a viewer who has scrolled the chapters
will feel the plates enacting the same argument the page makes, without one
caption saying so.

Color is doctrine, not decoration. The ground is graphite with a violet
undertone, never pure black, because the movement's darkness is a lit room
with the lights low. Violet exists only as volume: soft radial luminance that
gives the void depth and never becomes a surface. Magenta exists only as
signal: a small census of thin threads that follow the field at higher
fidelity than the beams, glowing because they carry the frame's meaning. Bone
white is reserved for the brightest edges of the brightest beams. On paper
plates the entire system inverts into ink: the same trajectories drawn as fine
graphite lines with no glow at all, drafting-table honest.

The craft standard is absolute: every plate must look like the product of a
master of computational aesthetics who tuned it across countless iterations,
because it effectively is; the parameters below were refined until each
failure mode disappeared. Stroke weight follows depth so the frame keeps
atmospheric perspective. Dash gaps follow a Poisson rhythm so breakage never
bands. Luminance accumulates additively and is then tone-mapped, so crossings
bloom the way long exposures do instead of clipping. Grain is applied last, at
film strength, so flat regions never posterize on real displays. A meticulously
crafted plate passes one test: hold it beside a photograph and it should feel
like it was shot, on instruments, inside a machine.

Emergence is the payoff of restraint. The algorithm never composes; it only
legislates: how many carriers, how strongly the field bends them, where light
is allowed to pool, when a stroke may break. Composition then emerges from the
interaction of law and seed, and the artist's work, the painstaking part, is
choosing the laws and then choosing among seeds like contact sheets. Each
chapter of the page receives its own legislation: interference for the problem,
a faint convergence for the ground beneath the wake-up, lateral flow for the
range, strata for the operating cadence, full assembly for proof. One movement,
one palette, five weathers.
